How it locks

Wood on wood. Nothing to unscrew.

Three simple joints hold everything together. There's no metal hardware anywhere, so there's nothing to lose, strip or tighten.

Joint one

Blocks stack on couplers

A square wooden coupler on top of every block seats into a pocket in the next. Stack two for a tall table, use one for a low one.

Joint two

Beams drop into notches

The beams sit into notches at the top of each leg. They square the frame and stop it racking before the top goes on.

Joint three

Planks lock edge to edge

Each plank has a self-locking edge and pockets underneath that seat over the legs, so the surface lies flat and stays put.

Questions

Good to know.

Do I need tools to change it?

No. Every joint is wood on wood: couplers between the leg blocks, notches for the beams and self-locking plank edges. You rebuild it by hand, and no furniture-building experience is needed.

Is it really the same parts every time?

Yes. One set of solid-wood leg blocks, beams and planks makes every table. Smaller tables like the desk and coffee table use fewer of them, and the rest stack neatly together until you need them.

What's included?

Everything needed to build all five tables, including the walnut edge rails for the game table and lounge game table. The edge rails are part of the set, not an extra. The play mat is the only optional add-on.

What is it made of?

Solid-wood leg blocks and beams, walnut-finish planks and rails, and wooden couplers. There's no metal hardware.
